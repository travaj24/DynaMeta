## 1. Terms

- **Local BTBT rate**: U = A |E|^P exp(-B/|E|) D(n, p), the Kane field factor times the Hurkx factor D.
  "Field factor" means A |E|^P exp(-B/|E|).
- **Quadrature error**: the relative difference between the rate the scheme integrates and the integral of
  the rate of the continuous field the mesh represents. The check estimates this for the field factor only.
- **Element field**: in 2-D / 3-D the scheme uses one field per triangle / tetrahedron, the gradient of the
  potential interpolated linearly between the nodes (the "P1 gradient"). Its magnitude is `BTBT_Emag`.
- **Dual pieces**: DEVSIM splits each element into pieces, one per (element edge, end node): the simplex
  spanned by the node, the edge midpoint, the face circumcentres (3-D) and the element circumcentre. Each piece's
  volume is that edge's `ElementNodeVolume`, and the scheme puts the rate of the element, times the D of the
  node, on it. In 1-D the pieces are the two half edges.
- **Circumcentre**: the centre of the circle (sphere) through an element's vertices. For an edge it is the
  midpoint.
- **Stencil**: the set of neighbouring element samples a local fit uses.
- **ENO** (essentially non-oscillatory) choice: of two candidate fits, take the smoother one. It keeps a fit
  from spanning a kink of the field.
- **Richardson reference**: (4 I(h/2) - I(h)) / 3, the extrapolation of a second-order quantity to h = 0 from
  two meshes. "Measured error" below is I(h) / I_ref - 1.
- **CV-averaged doping**: the doping at each node replaced by its average over the node's control volume, so
  the lumped charge of an oblique abrupt junction is right cell by cell.

## 2. What the 1-D estimator does (unchanged), and what has to carry over

`docs/audit/2026-11-06-v0.15-followups.md` item 1 has its derivation. In short:

1. The FV edge field is the continuum field at the edge MIDPOINT (Gauss's law with the lumped charge).
2. Each edge is split into its two halves, the parts of the two end nodes' control volumes. Within one control
   volume the lumped charge is constant, so the field is smooth there; kinks sit at the midpoints.
3. On each half the field is the quadratic through three midpoint samples, the ENO choice of the outward
   stencil (the edge and two beyond the node) and the inward one (one behind, the edge, one beyond).
4. f is integrated on each half by 16-point Gauss, D held at the scheme's value, and
   error = Sum (S - C) / Sum C, with S the scheme's share of each half and C the continuum share.

## 3. The 2-D / 3-D estimator (derivation)

The scheme's integral on a 2-D / 3-D region is

    I_h = Sum_T Sum_(edge e of T) Sum_(end k of e) |U_k,e| ENV_k,e,    U_k,e = A e_T^P exp(-B/e_T) D_k,

with e_T = |E_T| the element field magnitude and ENV_k,e the dual piece of (e, k). The estimator keeps all four
1-D ingredients and gives each its natural 2-D / 3-D form.

**(a) Where the element field is exact.** The P1 gradient of a potential is exact for a linear potential. For a
field that varies LINEARLY along its own direction n (a planar quadratic potential: uniformly doped depletion),
the P1 gradient's component along n is the continuum field at one point along n. With s_i = n . v_i the vertex
positions along n and g_i = n . grad(lambda_i) (lambda_i the barycentric coordinates),

    n . grad I(s^2) = Sum_i s_i^2 g_i = 2 t*,   so   t* = (1/2) Sum_i s_i^2 g_i,

since Sum_i g_i = 0 and Sum_i s_i g_i = 1 (P1 reproduces linear functions). t* is translation-covariant. It is the
midpoint on an edge (the 1-D sample), the cell centre on a right triangle / Kuhn tetrahedron of a tensor mesh
with an axis-aligned field, and the mid-plane on ANY simplex whose vertices lie on two planes normal to n.
Across the field the sample is put at the circumcentre (the Voronoi vertex the 1-D midpoint generalises),
clamped into the element. On a non-aligned field this differs from the circumcentre: on a right triangle with a
45 deg field, t* is at 0.35 h from the right-angle vertex along n, the circumcentre at 0.71 h. The two triangles
of a cell then sample at 0.35 h and 1.06 h. That is the "ripple" of P1 gradients on oblique fields, and placing
the samples there removes it.

  Why not the circumcentre alone: on a jittered Delaunay mesh (below) it under-predicted the measured error by
  24 % (estimate/measured 0.76), because the circumcentres of the two-plane triangles scatter along the field by
  ~0.1 h. With t* the ratio is 1.07, the same as on the tensor mesh (MEASURED). Why not the centroid: it misses the
  exact lateral oracle by 8.1e-3 (adversarial gate) and moves the corner ratio out of the band (0.70).

**(b) Pieces.** The scheme's own dual pieces, the 1-D half edges' analogue, are grouped by node: the pieces of
node k form the part of the element inside node k's control volume. Each piece is integrated with the D of its
own node (|U_k,e| / f(e_T)), as the scheme weights it. Signed pieces (a non-well-centred element after
`apply_exact_geometry`) are used with their signs, so the decomposition stays exact.

**(c) The continuum field on the pieces of node k.** This is a least-squares polynomial in field-aligned
coordinates (u along n, v [, w] across): quadratic along the field, linear across it, plus the cross terms
[1, u, v, (w), u^2, u v, (u w)]. The ENO choice is between two stencils.

- **Centred stencil** (the 1-D inward one): the element and its vertex neighbours (the 1-ring).
- **One-sided stencil** (the 1-D outward one): the samples of the vertex 2-ring that lie on node k's side along
  the field, plus those level with the element.

Take the centred fit only if its curvature along the field (|c_uu|) is strictly smaller, the 1-D order. Fall
back to a linear fit (1-ring, k's side) where neither quadratic is determined along the field, and to the
constant where k has no element beyond it. A node level with the sample uses the centred stencil.

- The transverse curvature is left out. Across the field the rate varies slowly, so its curvature enters the
  element quadrature at third order.
- A transverse direction is fitted only where the stencil has >= 2 levels across it. Otherwise the column is
  near-null, and noise in it was MEASURED to blow a 2 nm corner boundary element up to C/S = 7e10 before this
  rule existed.
- An overshoot guard rejects a candidate fit whose values on the element leave the stencil's data range widened
  by that range; the next fallback is then used. A smooth peak between samples overshoots far less. MEASURED on
  the 2 nm corner: it rejects 138 of the 3576 one-sided candidates, and the estimate is the same to all 17 digits
  with the guard disabled, so it is a safety net, not a tuning.

**(d) Integration.** f is integrated on every piece by a collapsed (Duffy / Stroud) Gauss rule, 16^2 points in 2-D
and 8^3 in 3-D, exact to degree 30 / 13. The quadratic is evaluated at the points through its values at the
piece's P2 nodes, which is exact, and turns the evaluation into one matrix product. Then

    error = Sum_pieces (S - C) / Sum_pieces C,   S = |U_k,e| ENV_k,e,   C = ENV_k,e mean_piece(|U_k,e| f(e_rec)/f(e_T)),

the 1-D formula, with the same bounded log-ratio form (`_log_f_ratio`, now the one kernel of both).

**Reduction to 1-D.** Take a laterally uniform state on a tensor (2-D) or Kuhn (3-D) mesh. Then n = x, every
sample sits at its column midpoint, the node pieces of a column fill exactly its two x-halves, and the
x-only data make the fit the 1-D quadratic through three column samples. The ENO ordering is the 1-D one. So the
estimate IS the 1-D one up to the two Gauss rules and round-off. MEASURED (Q1): 2-D minus 1-D +1.7e-15 /
-6.7e-15 / +2.1e-15 for the R1 uniform / long / parabola fields; 3-D -1.6e-15 / +1.4e-15.

**Skipped elements.** An element is skipped when its scheme share, and a bound on its continuum share, are both
below 1e-16 of the gross rate. The bound uses D at its largest end value and the field at 1.25 x the largest
over the 2-ring, since f is increasing in E. A skipped element counts as exact (C = S). Its effect on the
estimate is at most 1e-16 times the number of elements.

**Cost (MEASURED).**

- Per call: 0.2 s at 3721 nodes, 1.0 s at 14641, 4.9 s at 58081 (2-D corner; 1788 / 6987 / 27914 elements
  evaluated).
- Per evaluated element: ~0.15 ms per triangle and ~0.7 ms per tetrahedron (3-D Kuhn bar, 1656 tets in 1.1 s).
- The layout read (`devsim_geometry._Region`, the checked element-edge-to-node map) runs once per region and is
  cached while the element list is unchanged.
- For a long bias sweep, check once and pass `check_resolution=False` afterwards (docstring).

**3-D.** The same code with the 3-D pieces (two tetrahedra per (edge, node), weighted by their signed share of
the ENV, via the new `devsim_geometry.tetrahedron_dual_parts`) and a 3-D frame (v = the coordinate axis least
aligned with n, orthogonalised; w = n x v). The 3-D estimator is the 1-D one on lateral states (Q1). On the solved
Kuhn-bar Zener it equals the 2-D estimate: +3.1075e-2 against the 2-D +3.107494e-2 (Q3). So 3-D is checked by
default too. Not measured in 3-D: a genuinely 3-D field such as a corner, because of the cost of a converged
reference (INFERRED to behave like 2-D, since the construction is dimension-generic).

**Why not the alternatives.**

- A Richardson estimate needs a second solve.
- A bare h <= 0.35/k rule misses one long element on the peak (the 1-D finding).
- Element-centroid samples: rejected above.
- A full 2-D quadratic (including transverse curvature) has near-null columns on thin or boundary stencils
  (rejected above).
- No ENO (centred everywhere) misses the exact lateral oracle by 1.24e-2 (uniform) / 4.5e-2 (long). It fits
  across the abrupt junction's cusp (adversarial gate).

## 4. The band, and why

The estimate covers the field-factor quadrature. The total mesh error also has the discretisation of the field
and of the carriers in D. MEASURED on abrupt Si Zeners, those are O(h^2) of the OPPOSITE sign at moderate bias,
so the estimate exceeds the total.

- 1-D planar: estimate/measured 1.07 (0.9 V), 1.17 (1.5 V).
- 2-D corner: 1.19 / 1.34.
- 1-D note's fixtures: 1.26-1.4.

The gates require **1 <= predicted / measured <= 2** where the quadrature dominates (moderate bias). The lower
bound says the alarm is never optimistic there. The upper bound 2 is the factor beyond which a 1 % alarm would
fire on meshes whose true error is below 0.5 %. Mesh decisions are made on halvings of h, i.e. factors of 4
in the error, so a factor-2 estimate still picks the right mesh.

At high current (2.5 V here) the uncovered parts cancel the quadrature part. The total is then a small residue,
and the ratio is meaningless (1-D: 3.0, 8.4, then the measured error changes sign; corner: 3.3, 8.7). There the
estimate is conservative, |measured| <= estimate, which holds at every mesh measured. The band is not applied.

## 6. What the check does NOT cover (MEASURED)

1. **Field and carrier discretisation**, O(h^2), opposite sign at moderate bias (section 4). The check is
   conservative there.
2. **An abrupt junction that crosses the mesh obliquely.** The mesh error there is the representation of the
   abrupt doping on the oblique mesh, not the quadrature. The check does not see it, and it can be the dominant
   error. Device: the corner doping slanted by 30 deg across an axis-aligned tensor mesh, 120 x 40 nm, 0.9 V,
   against the CV-averaged 0.5 / 0.25 nm Richardson reference.

   | h | CV-averaged doping: measured / estimate | node-sampled (staircase): measured / estimate |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 nm | -4.65 % / +0.54 % (silent) | +0.86 % / +0.87 % |
   | 0.5 nm | -0.26 % / +0.22 % | +1.15 % / +0.25 % (silent) |
   | 0.25 nm | -0.07 % / +0.07 % | +0.31 % / (not re-run with the final estimator) |

   The staircase errors do not fall with h in a clean power law. The CV-averaged doping rounds the field cusp
   over ~h, a first-order effect in the peak field (INFERRED from the -4.65 % at 1 nm falling 18x by 0.5 nm).
   - Pinned by `test_q6_limitation_abrupt_junction_across_the_mesh_is_not_covered`.
   - Advice (docstring and module header): align mesh lines with abrupt junctions. A junction aligned with a
     rotated mesh is covered exactly (Q5), as is a corner on a tensor mesh (Q4).
3. **Model error.** The nonlocal note's corner flank (+8-20 %) and the planar 12.8x at 0.9 V are differences
   between the local and nonlocal MODELS, not mesh errors. On that study's 0.5 nm mesh the local quadrature error
   is +3.4 % (estimate) / +2.9 % (measured), and both models and the 1-D planar reference run at the same h.
   So the ratios carry at most part of it, and the conclusions stand.

## Release-note lines

- `btbt_generation_current(check_resolution=True)` (the default) now checks 2-D and 3-D regions too. It warns
  (`UnreliableResultWarning`, naming the region and the worst element) when the estimated quadrature error of
  the local BTBT current is above 1 %. The value is unchanged. On a laterally uniform device it is the 1-D
  estimate; on the junction corner where a 2 nm mesh was +40 % off with no warning, it now predicts +48 %.
- It does not cover an ABRUPT junction that crosses the mesh obliquely (the doping representation, -4.7 %
  at 1 nm with no warning, measured). Align mesh lines with abrupt junctions.
- Convergence studies on coarse meshes pass `check_resolution=False` explicitly. The check costs ~0.15 ms per
  triangle and ~0.7 ms per tetrahedron carrying the rate.
