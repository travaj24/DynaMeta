Branch `wip/v016_p20_leak` from the v0.16 staging tip 3b75599 (P1-P13, P16, P17 merged). Owner-requested addition

## 1. Question and formulation

A gated film a few nanometres thick, contacted only at its edge, is solved by the equilibrium physics with ONE Fermi
level per region (P5/P11: `fermi_ref_param`, set by the edge electrode). That is exact only without current. A gate
current through the thin dielectrics must flow laterally through the resistive sheet to the edge contact, so the
film's Fermi level phi_F varies along the film and the gate-to-film voltage that sets the accumulation is smaller
than the applied one away from the contact.

**Chosen: option A, quasi-equilibrium film with a laterally varying Fermi level.** The film keeps the equilibrium
closure (parabolic or Kane), but phi_F (E_F = -q phi_F) becomes a node solution governed by current continuity:

    div j = 0,  j = -q mu n grad phi_F      in the film          (FermiLevelEquation)
    sink -A_i J_g,i on each film node of the face under gate g   (the gate current, J > 0 into the film)
    phi_F = V_c at the edge electrode; zero normal current elsewhere

j is the electron current in quasi-Fermi form (drift + diffusion with the Fermi-Dirac Einstein relation), so this is
the exact unipolar transport of the equilibrium film. Integrated over the thickness it is the distributed-resistor
sheet equation -d/dx(sigma_s dphi_F/dx) = J_top + J_bot, sigma_s = q mu Int n dz, with the vertical variation of
phi_F reported (`vertical_spread_V`). The current enters through one face and spreads over the thickness, so phi_F(z)
is a parabola with peak-to-peak J t / (2 q mu n) and the face it crosses lies 2/3 of that above the column mean; relative
to the lateral drop of a film contacted at one end, J L^2 / (2 sigma_s), that is t^2 / L^2 (4 t^2 / L^2 with two
contacts): 1.2e-4 in the 0.5 um 2-D cross-check of section 5.6 (t^2/L^2 = 1.4e-4), 3.6e-7 over 10 um (equal to the
formula), but 2.1 % in a 40 nm cell, where the sheet picture is no longer a good summary. Implemented with the node solution named like the C2 parameter
(`EF_ref_V`), so the closure strings, their precomputed derivative and every eta consumer (film interface traps) are
unchanged; Poisson gains d(charge)/d phi_F = -d(charge)/d psi (every charge term of an equilibrium film is a function
of eta or a constant, checked at setup).

**Why not option B (the film as a unipolar drift-diffusion region with the existing tunnelling models).** What the
library has (section 2) is a self-consistent tunnelling term that REFUSES anything but a bipolar drift-diffusion
region (it needs an electron AND a hole continuity row); running the degenerate film as bipolar DD (n ~ 1e26-1e27
m^-3, a wide-gap oxide: p ~ 0) is numerically ill-posed, and the DD path does not carry the equilibrium path's edge Fermi
reference, Kane closure, film traps and native screening mesh. Option A keeps all of those and adds one unknown per
film node. B would also need edits to `tunneling.py` (not owned here).

**What the model represents and what it does not.**
- Covered: direct and Fowler-Nordheim tunnelling of conduction-band electrons through the dielectric STACK (one
  two-sided Tsu-Esaki/WKB formula for both regimes and both directions, zero at equal Fermi levels), lateral ohmic
  transport in the film, any number of gates and edge contacts (contacts at different voltages now allowed: a current
  flows), composition with fixed charges, work functions, film interface traps and the Kane band.
- Not covered: trap-assisted tunnelling, Poole-Frenkel and hopping conduction, hot carriers, hole / valence-band
  tunnelling, image-force lowering, dielectric breakdown, self-heating. **Real high-k films at several MV/cm are often
  dominated by trap-assisted / Poole-Frenkel conduction, which a direct / Fowler-Nordheim model UNDER-ESTIMATES**; for
  those use `model="table"` with measured data (`LeakageTable(V, J)` or a callable `V -> (J, dJ/dV)`, where V is the
  local gate-to-film Fermi-potential difference; or `LeakageTable.from_field(E, J, t)`, read at the solved drop across
  the dielectric column).
- Density gradient: REFUSED on the same film (its quantum potential shifts the supply and its Lambda equation does not
  follow a varying Fermi level). 3-D: REFUSED (the 3-D builder has no lateral Fermi field; `setup_fermi_field_region`
  raises in 1-D and 3-D). Noise / small-signal: not modelled (the term has no noise model, so `noise_analysis` refuses
  the device). The Tsu-Esaki supply uses a parabolic transverse mass (the film's DOS mass; for a Kane band its
  band-edge mass).

  `TunnelLayer`, `DielectricBarrier`, the column helpers, `custom_terms` (the nonlocal-term mechanism),
  `physics_traps.interface_node_area` (the interface area each node owns). No second tunnelling formula was written.

FINDING (DEVSIM, MEASURED): a node that lies on both a contact and an interface is given to the CONTACT only; the
interface then lacks that node, and Potential continuity is lost there. With the edge contact over the film's full z
range, the film/oxide interface lost its x = 0 corner node and the gate current of the first interior column was
~3x the bulk value (MEASURED on a gated degenerate film of this kind). The leak contact is therefore inset by 1e-12 m from both faces. The same
mechanism applies to the existing drift-diffusion full-edge strips (corner node of the film/oxide interface at x = w);
not changed here (outside this package) -- recorded as a request below.

### Public API (usage)

```python
from dynameta.carriers.devsim_layered import LayeredDevsimBuilder
from dynameta.carriers.gate_leakage import GateLeakage, LeakageTable
from dynameta.carriers.tunneling import DielectricBarrier
from dynameta.geometry import Electrode

design.electrodes += [Electrode("gnd", "film", "x_lo", role="ground")]     # the edge contact
oxb = DielectricBarrier(2.5, "TCO", 0.15)            # conduction-band offset above the TCO conduction edge [eV]
oxa = DielectricBarrier(3.4, "TCO", 0.30)
leak = GateLeakage(model="tsu_esaki", barriers={"up_oxb": oxb, "lo_oxb": oxb, "up_oxa": oxa, "lo_oxa": oxa},
                   gate_fermi_energy_eV=10.0, energy_max_eV=8.0)      # mobility: the TransportModel's at n_bg
# measured leakage instead: GateLeakage(model="table", table=LeakageTable.from_field(E_V_m, J_A_m2, 8e-9))
b = LayeredDevsimBuilder(design, gate_leakage=leak)
cf = b.solve(BiasPoint({"top": 3.0, "bot": 0.0}, "x"), abs_tol=1e-14)
rep = cf.extras["gate_leakage"]["film"]       # gates {I, J_mean, gate_action}, contacts, balance, phi_F_V(x),
                                              # max_drop_V, vertical_spread_V, P_joule, P_tunnel, P_terminals
# films too long to mesh: gate_leakage.solve_lateral_1d(L, sigma_s(phi), J_net(phi))
```

## 5. Parameter study: lateral drop of a gated 6 nm film and the leakage needed to reduce the gate action

1-D cross-check), MEASURED on the gated-film cell of `tests/_wiring_fixture.py`: film n = 3e26
m^-3 (E_F - E_c = 0.544 eV with DOS mass 0.30 m0), 6 nm, between 6 nm oxB (eps 20) + 2 nm oxA (eps 9) on each face
(oxB next to the film), metal gates with the film's work function (flat band at 0 V), mobility 30 cm^2/Vs; top gate
+-3 V, bottom gate +-5 V. The low / high barrier sets and the two-gate cases are printed by the `--full` run and are not recorded here
("--").

| 30 cm^2/Vs | 0.865 mS | 1156 ohm/sq |
| 40 cm^2/Vs | 1.154 mS | 867 ohm/sq |

### 5.6 Cross-check of the reduction (the validation script's exit gate)

2-D DEVSIM, 0.5 um film, bottom gate +5 V, central set (the strongest current, 2.1e7 A/m^2): drop 0.00243 V, gate
action 0.99968; the reduced 1-D model on the narrow-cell tables: the same to 4.2e-5 (drop) and 5.4e-4 (1 - action)
relative; currents balance to 8.4e-17; vertical spread 2.8e-7 V (1.2e-4 of the drop; t^2/L^2 = 1.4e-4).

### 5.7 Reading

For this film and edge contacts 10-1000 um from the gated area, reducing the gate action to a fraction between 0.9
and 0.5 by gate leakage needs 0.09-8e3 A/cm^2 at the nominal bias even for a bias-independent leakage (estimate (a),
the lower bound), and orders of magnitude more for any field-activated mechanism (estimate (b); a gate action of 0.5
is often unreachable at all). With the central barrier set the top gate's own tunnelling current (~1e-5 A/cm^2 at
+-3 V) gives a lateral drop below 0.4 mV at 1 mm; the only case with a noticeable drop is the bottom gate at +5 V
(9 MV/cm in the 2 nm oxA layer), 0.32 V at 10 um and 1.4 V (action 0.74) at 1 mm. A measured gate current goes
through `LeakageTable` (it captures trap-assisted conduction the tunnelling model does not; a J(E) curve through
`LeakageTable.from_field`, read at the solved dielectric drop); by the table above, a measured
J below ~0.09 A/cm^2 at L <= 1 mm keeps the gate action above 0.9 at the top-gate bias.
