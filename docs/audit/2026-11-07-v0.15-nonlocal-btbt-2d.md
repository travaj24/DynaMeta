## 2. Design

* **Starts and direction.**
  * Every interior, non-contact node with a nonzero gradient is a start.
  * Dynamic mode (the default): the path runs along the start's recovered nodal gradient of psi, i.e.
    along -grad E_V, so the path turns as the solution changes.
  * Frozen mode: `NonlocalBTBT(direction=(dx, dy))`.
  * |grad E_V| at the start is the recovered gradient's norm.
* **Walker.**
  * At each crossing it re-solves the exit point exactly: a 2 x 2 system on the edge, giving s and mu.
  * Exits are found from barycentric coordinates.
  * A ray that hits a vertex (tolerance 1e-10), or runs along an edge, is continued by a sector search
    around that vertex.
  * Every path point is an edge point (n1, n2, mu), so any nodal field there is (1-mu) X[n1] + mu X[n2].
  * Two passes: count, then fill. The walker also returns ds/dtheta and dmu/dtheta at every point.
* **Path ends.** The end is the exact linear root E_C = eps inside the last triangle.
  * A path that leaves the region is discarded, whether at a Neumann side, an interface or a contact.
    Decision 24: no reflection.
  * A path is also discarded if it bends back (reentrant), ends next to a contact node, or exceeds
    `max_length` (with the 1-D taper).
  * Each discard is counted in `last_stats`.
* **Physics: identical to 1-D.**
  * The same closed-form segment integrals, the band-variable prefactor moments, the occupancies and the
    sinh form of f_n - f_p.
  * Holes go to the start node's row. Electrons go to the end point's triangle, spread over its nodes with
    the barycentric weights. Conservation is exact by construction.
  * Noise: one two-point source per path (`btbt_nl`), with a hole row at the start and electron rows at the
    end-triangle nodes.

## 3. Gradient recovery

The start field, the path direction and the prefactor's nodal slopes all need a nodal gradient.

* The one-element (P1) gradient is first order on a non-aligned mesh: order 1.29, measured in R1. The
  area-weighted average of the elements measured 1.91 on the R1 meshes (superconvergence on their regular
  patches), so it is not used as the refutation. It is not second order on general meshes either.
* The gradient used: a **least-squares quadratic fit over the 1-ring** of each node.
  * The xy monomial is dropped when the fit is rank-deficient. This happens at the valence-4 nodes of a
    union-jack mesh.
  * The fit falls back to the 2-ring when the 1-ring is too small.
* **Boundary nodes.**
  * Homogeneous-Neumann boundary nodes get a mirror image of the ring (the field there is tangential).
  * Contact and interface nodes, and every other boundary node, are fitted one-sided.
  * The first version mirrored every boundary node, which gave wrong gradients where the field crosses the
    boundary. Fixed with the `neumann_nodes` mask.
* **Consequence.** On a tensor mesh with a laterally uniform psi, the recovery is EXACTLY the 1-D 3-point
  formula. This is why gate L2 holds to round-off and not just to O(h^2).

## 4. Jacobian: exact, including the dynamic direction (decision 25)

Decision 25 expected an approximate Jacobian in dynamic mode, with ssac and noise refused. The exact one
proved affordable, so **nothing is refused**: the term declares `jacobian_exact = True` in 2-D too.

* The path angle is theta = atan2(g_start), with g = (Gx psi, Gy psi) from the recovery operators.
  * d theta/d psi = (d_perp . d g_start)/|g_start|.
  * A rotation moves every path point along its own edge, giving ds/dtheta and dmu/dtheta from the walker's
    2 x 2 system.
* The chain rule then runs through:
  * the point values;
  * the prefactor slopes, which depend on the direction itself;
  * the segment lengths (complex step in L);
  * the end point's occupancy and its barycentric deposition weights (`e_dwdth`).
* **Vertex passages.** A ray through a node is not differentiable in theta: the one-sided derivatives
  differ. This is a set of measure zero, and 0 is used there.
* **Cost.** On the 0.25 nm mesh, the Jacobian adds about 5x to the rate evaluation (2.0 s to 11.0 s;
  section 6).

Other measurements on this device:
* **Shares of the total generation.** Within 12 nm of the corner: 0.3-0.5 %. Within 25 nm of the corner
  (both arms' bands): 8.6-11 %. The rest is on the arms beyond c + 25 nm.
* **Per-length check.** The nonlocal generation per arm length in the far window is 1.763 / 79.1 /
  2676 A/m^2. The 1-D values are 1.796 / 82.9 / 2973.
  * The deficit (-2 / -5 / -10 %) is also in the local formula (-3 / -5 / -8 %). It is therefore the ±15 nm
    band not holding the whole depletion width at high bias, not the model. This is inferred, and it does
    not enter the ratio.
* **Mirror symmetry.** The two arms agree to <= 0.5 %.
* **Conservation.** The nonlocal electron and hole totals are equal to round-off.
* **Carriers leave the corner zone.** In the zone within 25 nm of the corner (corner plus flanks), the
  electron total is 0.81-0.82 of the hole total. So about 19 % of the electrons from paths starting there
  land outside it, further into the n quadrant. The arms receive 1.7-2.7 % more electrons than holes.

**Answer: where the LOCAL model errs most in 2-D compared to 1-D.** On this Si corner the answer has four
parts, ranked by size.

1. **The error that is not 2-D at all is still the largest.** The local model's over-prediction depends on
   bias: 12.8x at 0.9 V, 3.8x at 1.5 V, 1.44x at 2.5 V on the planar parts. That is exactly the 1-D figure (to
   1-2 %), because 2-D geometry does not change the physics of a planar junction. Low-bias / onset currents
   (GIDL onset, TFET subthreshold) are where the local model is worst, in 1-D and 2-D alike.
2. **Mesh sensitivity on practical 2-D meshes, with no warning.**
   * On a uniform 2 nm 2-D mesh (3721 nodes), the local device's current is +36 / +21 / +5 % off its 0.5 nm
     value (0.9 / 1.5 / 2.5 V). The nonlocal current moves by <= 0.7 %.
   * Point-sampled, the local formula on the far arms reads -24 % (0.9 V) at 2 nm.
   * The local model's resolution check (`btbt_quadrature_error`, the 1 % warning) is **1-D only**: it
     returns None in 2-D, so this goes unflagged.
   * The nonlocal path integrals are closed forms along the path and have no such sampling error. On a
     coarse 2-D mesh this is the largest local-model error in practice.
   * Not shown: that the 2-D local integral is converged at 0.5 nm. A 0.25 nm corner run is ~4x the nodes
     and was not made.
3. **Geometry: errors of opposite sign next to the corner and at the corner.**
   * On the flanks 5-25 nm from the corner, the local model over-predicts by a further +8-20 % beyond its
     planar factor.
   * Within 12 nm of the corner it over-predicts 8-16 % LESS than planar.
   * Inferred mechanism: the local rate follows the enhanced nodal |grad psi| near the convex n corner
     exponentially. A tunnelling path integrates kappa over its whole length, through the region where the
     field relaxes, so it sees less of the enhancement. At the corner itself, diagonal paths collect the band
     bending of both arms, which no single nodal field represents.
   * These partly cancel in the integral. The whole-device error is only +4-7 % beyond planar (DEVSIM's own
     local integral), because the corner zone carries ~10 % of the current.
   * Sharper geometries (a gate edge with a thin oxide, a TFET source edge) put a larger share of the current
     near the edge. That is where this term grows (inferred: no such device was built).
4. **Where the carriers appear.** In 1-D the local model puts the electron at the hole's node, a few nm
   from where the nonlocal model puts it. In 2-D the separation is also LATERAL: 19 % of the electrons from
   paths that start within 25 nm of the corner land outside that zone. A local model cannot represent this.
   It matters wherever the electron's landing region is connected to a different terminal or gate than the
   hole's, which is the GIDL drain-vs-body split (inferred, not built).

**The 2-D nonlocal model is the reference** for these statements. Its own 2-D accuracy is gated: L2 (== 1-D
to round-off), R1 (second-order convergence to the continuum at 30 and 45 degrees), S2 (exact Jacobian, FDT),
and the far-arm planar check above.

## 9. Open items (not done)

* **Path reflection** at Neumann sides (decision 24): not implemented. Paths that leave the region are
  discarded and counted ("boundary": 60-68 of 3000-5000 paths on the corner device).
  * On a mirror-plane side the true answer is the reflected path. The discarded share is a model error
    there: small on the corner device, but unmeasured in general.
* **3-D:** refused (NotImplementedError). The walker generalises to tetrahedra, but the cost needs review
  first.
* **No gate-edge / GIDL device** was built: the corner is the only 2-D geometry measured. A MOS gate edge
  needs the oxide region, and paths stop at the interface (one region).
* **Vertex passages** are not differentiable in theta (measure zero; 0 is used). A path that grazes a node
  can make Newton less smooth. No effect was seen in any gate.
* **Planar 2-D reference device fails Poisson presolve.** The planar version of the corner device fails
  DEVSIM's Poisson presolve on the graded mesh (2 nm coarse, 0.5 nm fine). That is why the planar reference
  is the 1-D device; the 1-D reference is the stronger check anyway, being independent code.
* **Rate + Jacobian assembly** (numpy/scipy) dominates at >= 50k nodes (11 s per evaluation at 58k). A numba
  port of `_rates_core` is the next speed step.
* **Local model's 2-D resolution check** (finding, not changed): `btbt_quadrature_error` returns None in
  2-D/3-D. Section 7 item 2 measured the 2-D local current +36 % off at a uniform 2 nm mesh, with no warning.
  A 2-D estimator, or at least an AdvisoryWarning that the check is skipped, belongs to the local-BTBT owner.
* **CI time.** `solvers-devsim` grows by ~1-2 min (runner, inferred). Its estimate is now ~22-35 min against
  the 40-min cap, so the split already proposed in that job's comment is due if the next run exceeds
  ~30 min. Nightly (a2) adds ~18 min (dev box).

   * Cause: the product form sinh(d)/(2 cosh(za/2) cosh(zb/2)) overflows once |za| + |zb| exceeds ~1420.

   * Measured: the FD corner device's first solve stopped with `RuntimeWarning: overflow encountered in sinh`
     (a DEVSIM FATAL). Boltzmann activities never get there.
   * Fix: `_occupancy_difference`. It keeps the product form, bitwise, while |za| + |zb| <= 1000. Above that it
     uses the identity sinh(d)/(cosh s + cosh d), with numerator and denominator scaled by exp(-max(|s|, |d|))
     and the numerator written as -exp(|d| - m) expm1(-2|d|)/2. It is accurate for every |d| and exactly 0 at
     d = 0.
   * Gate N0d (`test_btbt_nonlocal_kernels.py`): <= 0.49 eps (1 + |za| + |zb|) against mpmath at 50 digits,
     for |z| <= 3000 including near-equilibrium pairs. Budget 2 eps (1 + |za| + |zb|). Bitwise equal to the
     old form below 1000. The old form raises the overflow warning on the large inputs.
2. **FD equilibrium from the seed is slow on the graded 2-D corner mesh.**
   * The bare FD device, with no BTBT term, needs 186 Newton steps from the seed at h = 0.5 nm. Boltzmann with
     the term needs 45; the local-model FD twin needs 21.
   * With the nonlocal term installed before the first solve, it does not converge in 400 steps.
   * The corner child now solves the FD equilibrium first and installs the term after (1 more step).
     Boltzmann keeps its recorded order.
   * Not investigated further: why the bare FD device is slower from the seed than its local-BTBT twin. That
     belongs to the FD-default owner.
3. **The child timeout was too tight.** The Boltzmann corner child took 891 s against `run_child`'s 900 s

## Release-note lines

These replace `<<DECISION PENDING: (c) ...>>` and the "2-D nonlocal BTBT is not built" known-limitation line
of `2026-11-06-v0.15.0-release.md`.

* **Nonlocal band-to-band tunnelling in 2-D** (`setup_btbt_nonlocal` on a 2-D bipolar region; numba
  required).
  * Paths run along the local field from every start node and turn with the solution (dynamic direction), or
    along a fixed direction (`NonlocalBTBT(direction=...)`).
  * Paths are traced exactly through the triangles. Holes appear at the path start, electrons at its end
    (spread over the end triangle).
  * The Jacobian is exact, including the path rotation, so ssac and noise (two-point, `btbt_nl`) are
    supported. Decision 25's refusal was not needed.
  * Laterally uniform devices give the 1-D result to round-off. Rotated junctions on non-aligned meshes
    converge at second order (1.93-2.00).
  * No path reflection (decision 24). 3-D is refused.
* **Measured on a Si corner junction.**
  * The local Hurkx model's planar error (12.8x at 0.9 V, 1.44x at 2.5 V) is unchanged in 2-D.
  * Next to the corner it over-predicts by a further 8-20 %, and at the corner 8-16 % less.
  * On a 2 nm 2-D mesh the local current is up to 36 % off with no warning, while the nonlocal model moves
    by < 1 %.
* 2-D nonlocal BTBT is gated under the FD default statistics too (degenerate slanted diode: zero current at
  equilibrium to round-off, FDT, ssac, Jacobian incl. the carrier columns; `statistics="legacy"` fails them),
  and `f_n - f_p` no longer overflows for FD-sized log activities.
