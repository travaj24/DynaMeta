# DynaMeta v0.15.1 -- release note (2026-11-08)

On top of v0.15.0 (`2026-11-06-v0.15.0-release.md`). Each section below is one addition. No existing result moves. One default changes: the local-BTBT
resolution check (`btbt_generation_current(check_resolution=True)`) now also runs in 2-D and 3-D, so an
under-resolved 2-D/3-D tunnelling peak now warns (values are unchanged; the 2-D/3-D BTBT section). The valley-resolved
electron density gradient and the Si(100)-calibrated gamma (last section) are opt-in; no default changes.

## optics.soa: opt-in C-band (1550 nm) SOA material

(`2026-11-08-v0.15-soa-cband.md`; user decision 2026-11-08)

- **`BulkGainParams.c_band()`** is a bulk SOA whose active region is InGaAsP lattice-matched to InP:
  Ga0.440 In0.560 As0.937 P0.063.
  - **Source:** Vurgaftman, Meyer & Ram-Mohan, J. Appl. Phys. 89, 5815 (2001), Eq. (5.4) and
    Sec. V.B.1. The alloy is built from the InP law and the Ga0.47In0.53As law with a bowing of
    0.13 eV. Its temperature dependence comes from the binary Varshni laws, as the paper recommends.
  - **Gap:** 0.768 eV at 300 K.
  - **Gain peak (measured):** 1549.7 nm at the reference drive (130 mA, 600 um, 298 K).
  - **Device gain:** 26.2 dB at alpha_i = 3000 /m, or 27.5 dB at 2500 /m.
  - **Drift:** 0.68 nm/K from the gap alone, 1.00 nm/K at fixed current.
  - The composition was solved to put the peak at 1550 nm and then rounded to 0.001. The peak still
    moves with drive current and temperature.
  - Pass other fields as overrides, e.g. `BulkGainParams.c_band(T_K=320.0)`.
- **The default `BulkGainParams()` is unchanged, bit for bit.** It is still the In0.53Ga0.47As L-band
  device, with its peak at 1612.5 nm (1182-value hash census, v0.15.0 against v0.15.1).
- **This replaces the v0.14.0 advice** for C-band work, which was to pass
  `Eg0_eV=0.816, varshni_alpha_eV_K=2.9e-4, varshni_beta_K=193.0`. That triple has no source. Its gap
  slope is 30 % low, so its drift is 0.48 nm/K gap-only. Its peak is at 1560.4 nm. It still works as a
  device-effective override.
- **New gap-law names:**
  - `ingaasp_lm_gap_law(z)` for any InP-matched GaInAsP composition (z is the As fraction);
  - the material key `"InGaAsP_LM_C"`;
  - `ingaasp_lattice_mismatch`, `LATTICE_CONSTANT_300K_A`, `LATTICE_MATCH_RTOL`, `INGAASP_LM_C_Z`,
    `INGAASP_LM_BOWING_EV` in `dynameta.materials.band_temperature`.

  `ingaasp_lm_gap_law` is also exported from `dynameta.materials` and `dynameta.optics.soa`.
- **`varshni_eg_ev` accepts lattice-matched quaternaries** (and so does everything built on it:
  `BandTemperature`, `qd_params_at_temperature`, `gain_peak_drift_nm_per_K`). One end point of the
  `(A, B, z, C)` spec may now be a ternary name. Such an end point must be lattice matched to the
  other one by Vegard's law, within 1e-3. A mismatched pair is refused with the mismatch quoted, as
  before.
- **Limits:**
  - Only the gap is specific to InGaAsP. The masses and recombination constants are the module's
    generic defaults.
  - No measured bulk-InGaAsP SOA drift was available to compare against. Measured InP-based 1550 nm
    quantum-dot and quantum-dash SOAs drift 0.77-0.78 nm/K. See the audit note.

## carriers: opt-in uniform mesh band at p-n junctions (2-D builder)

(`2026-11-08-v0.15-junction-mesh-band.md`; user decision 2026-11-08, branch `wip/v015_jband`)

- **`LayeredDevsimBuilder(design, junction_band=True)`** puts a uniform band of mesh lines around every p-n
  junction of the `bipolar_dd` regions: sign changes of an in-region `net_doping_expr` (found by probing the
  expression with DEVSIM) and z-stacked interfaces doped oppositely on the two sides.
  `carriers.junction_mesh.JunctionBand(half_width_m=, spacing_m=)` sets the band explicitly.
  `builder.junction_band_report` lists each junction found.
- **Defaults are physical:** half-width = the zero-bias depletion width; spacing = min(V_t / E_0, the BTBT
  rule h <= 0.35/k, the spacing of a line already on the junction). That is ~4 V_bi / V_t lines per junction.
- **Why:** DEVSIM's line grading leaves one irregular edge ending on the junction line. The band removes it.
  Measured: the GaN-like extended-precision diode current error goes from +4.95e-3 to -6.1e-4; a Ge Zener's
  BTBT current from +2.3e-2 to +3.1e-3 (graded at the same 0.32 nm spacing); depletion capacitance 10-50x
  better. On the band the error converges at clean second order.
- **Cost and limits:** 2-6x the nodes and solve time. It does not improve a diffusion-limited forward current,
  a node-sampled peak rate, z-stacked layer junctions, or devices without a junction (it then warns and changes
  nothing). The 3-D builder is not affected (different mesher, no remainder edge).
- **Off by default, byte-identical:** the mesh call log of five builder fixtures equals the digest recorded on
  v0.15.0, and solves are bitwise equal (gates D1 / D2 of `tests/test_devsim_junction_band.py`).

## Local BTBT resolution check in 2-D and 3-D (`wip/v015_btbt2dres`; `2026-11-08-v0.15-btbt-resolution-2d.md`)

User decision 2026-11-08: extend the resolution warning to 2-D ("it allows more generality and is applicable and
necessary").

- **Default change (warning only, no value moves).** `btbt_generation_current(..., check_resolution=True)` (the
  default) now estimates the quadrature error of the local BTBT current on 2-D and 3-D regions too
  (`tunneling.btbt_quadrature_error`, which returned None there). Above 1 % it warns
  (`UnreliableResultWarning`, naming the region and the worst element, with its extent along the field and
  k h). The returned current is the same expression as before; bitwise A/B with and without the check, the
  following solves included.
- **What it estimates.** The same quantity as in 1-D: the error of sampling the field factor A E^P exp(-B/E) once
  per element. The 2-D / 3-D form uses the scheme's own dual pieces and element samples, with a quadratic along
  the field (ENO choice of a centred and a one-sided stencil).
  - On a laterally uniform device it IS the 1-D estimate (1e-14, measured).
  - It does not depend on the mesh orientation (4e-13 on a rotated mesh).
  - It works on tensor, Kuhn and unstructured (exact-geometry) meshes.
- **Measured on the nonlocal study's junction corner.**
  - The uniform 2 nm mesh, where v0.15.0 reported the local current "+36 % off, no warning", is +40.3 % off a
    mesh-converged reference. It now warns, with a prediction of +48.4 %.
  - Predicted / measured is 1.18-1.20 (0.9 V) and 1.31-1.35 (1.5 V) at 2, 1 and 0.5 nm. The 1-D planar values
    are 1.07 / 1.17. The estimate is conservative because the uncovered field and carrier errors have the
    opposite sign.
  - At high current (2.5 V) the uncovered errors cancel most of it: |error| <= estimate there.
- **Cost.** ~0.15 ms per triangle and ~0.7 ms per tetrahedron carrying the rate (4.9 s on a 58k-node 2-D device),
  once per call. Check once, then pass `check_resolution=False` inside long sweeps.
- **Callers changed.** Two coarse-by-design 2-D cases now pass `check_resolution=False` explicitly: the 2-D L1
  convergence study in `tests/test_devsim_tunneling.py`, and the corner measurement's local twin in
  `tests/_btbt_nonlocal_child.py`. On that study's 0.5 nm mesh the local current is +2.9 % off (measured; the
  estimate is +3.4 %).
- **Updated limitation** (replaces the v0.15.0 note's "the resolution check is 1-D only" in sections 4.2 and 12):
  - The check covers 1-D, 2-D and 3-D.
  - It does not cover the field and carrier discretisation (as in 1-D).
  - It does not cover an ABRUPT junction that crosses the mesh obliquely. There the representation of the doping
    on the oblique mesh is the leading mesh error: measured -4.65 % at 1 nm with an estimate of +0.54 %, no
    warning. Align mesh lines with abrupt junctions; an aligned junction is covered, rotated mesh included.
  - 3-D is checked with the same construction, validated on laterally uniform devices only; no 3-D corner was
    measured.
- **API.** `btbt_quadrature_error` returns, in 2-D / 3-D: `worst_element_m`, `worst_centre_m`, `worst_h_m` (extent
  along the field), `worst_k_per_m`, `worst_kh`, `max_neighbour_log_ratio`, `elements_evaluated` (the 1-D keys are
  unchanged). New public helpers `devsim_geometry.tetrahedron_dual_parts` and `tetrahedron_circumcentres`.
  `tetrahedron_dual_pieces` is now built on them, bitwise equal (measured).
- **Tests.** New `tests/test_devsim_btbt_resolution_2d.py` (on `solvers-devsim`), 18 fast tests, plus 2 @slow
  reference re-measurements (99 s / ~16 min, nightly).

## Correction: the anisotropic-Si SP number (documentation only)

- The v0.15.0 notes report that real Si(100) valleys move the Schrodinger-Poisson inversion charge by +41 %
  against the isotropic model (1.2 V). That number mixes two effects:
  - The valleys' classical-limit DOS is 1.372 x the drift-diffusion N_c, while the body Fermi level is set with
    the isotropic N_c.
  - With the mismatch removed (each g_v / 1.372), the shift is **+28.3 % (N_inv) / -22.4 % (centroid)**. That is
    the quantization (valley repopulation) part. Measured on this tree with `sp_inversion(..., valleys=...)`.
- The GATE G result (DG +10.5-11.1 % vs the isotropic SP) checks the DG against the model it represents. It is
  not a statement about Si.
- Corrected in place, marked "corrected 2026-11-08": `2026-11-06-v0.15-dg-wall-strong-inversion.md` and
  `2026-11-06-v0.15.0-release.md`. No code or gate changes.

## Test infrastructure: platform-independent digests and an SVD-floor gate (`wip/v0151_frag`)

(`2026-11-10-v0.15.1-digest-svd-fragility.md`; follow-up of `2026-11-08-v0.15-linux-ci-exactness.md`.)
Tests only; no library code changes, no result moves.

- **The other three call-log digests are canonical now.** The bipolar, DG and BTBT call logs (like the fdext
  log since 2026-11-08) are recorded with correctly rounded exp / log / pow / tanh (`tests/_cr_libm.py`), so
  a pinned digest no longer depends on the platform's C library or on numpy's own vectorized functions. One
  shared helper, `tests/_calllog.py`, replaces the four recorders' copies of the interceptor and digest.
- **Re-recorded on the reference commits** (273473b, e95a638, 8434cce, 66f6185) with the current recorder:
  equal on Windows and in the WSL CI proxy, and equal to this tree's. The bipolar and BTBT digests do not
  change (no hard rounding case in them). The two DG digests change: numpy's `np.tanh` misses 3 of the wall
  seed's 15 arguments by 0.57-0.68 ulp, the same way on both platforms, which the seed amplifies to up to
  433726 ulp in 9 literals. The old raw digests are kept in comments.
- **Still bites:** mathematically equal 1-ulp changes (an ohmic-offset log split, an FD coefficient
  re-association, a DG seed log rewrite, the BTBT prefactor re-association) and text changes each change the
  canonical digests, the same way on both platforms.
- **Speed:** the FD-statistics bipolar log evaluates 1.39e6 transcendentals. A vectorized double-double fast
  path in `_cr_libm` decides all but 17 of them (0 differences from the decimal definition), so the log
  records in 19 s instead of 254 s. New `tests/test_cr_libm.py` gates the fast path.
- **G1-0 (`test_quasi_equilibrium.py::test_closure_removes_the_near_null_mode`) no longer divides by SVD
  round-off.** The full model's sigma_min (2.6e-16 on Windows, 4.6e-16 in WSL) is below the SVD's resolution;
  the old `closed / full >= 1e8` had a 1.1x margin in WSL. The gate now asserts, with the n eps budget of the
  well-posedness test (LAPACK backward stability + Weyl): full sigma_min / sigma_max <= n eps (measured
  4e-4 / 7e-4 of it), closed >= 100 n eps (measured 8.8e4 n eps), and the quasi-Fermi variables leave the full
  model at the floor (<= n eps; measured 1.2e-3 / 1.4e-3). Closure disabled, or s[-2] picked, fails it.
- **Not covered:** the C `pow()` behind `**` (import-time constants, `s ** 3` in `fd_potential_shift`) cannot
  be intercepted; it is bitwise equal on Windows and WSL today.

## carriers: opt-in valley-resolved electron density gradient and a Si(100)-calibrated gamma (`wip/v0151_vdg`)

(`2026-11-10-v0.15.1-valley-resolved-dg.md`; user decision 2026-11-10: both items opt-in. Research note behind
it: the 2026-11-08 gamma study.)

- **`setup_bipolar_dg(..., electron_valleys=SI100_ELECTRON_VALLEYS)`** (instead of `m_eff_n_kg`). Each valley set
  gets its own quantum potential with its own confinement mass: the 2 valleys with m_z = m_l = 0.916 m0 (share
  1/3 of N_c) and the 4 with m_z = m_t = 0.19 m0 (share 2/3). All sets share one electron quasi-Fermi level, so
  there is still one electron continuity row; its current uses an effective band-edge shift Lambda_eff,
  derived in the module (`QLambda` is then Lambda_eff). Walls, contacts, seed, gamma ramp and the
  quasi-equilibrium closure work as before. The valley table is data (`density_gradient.ValleySet`), so other
  orientations are other tables. Holes are unchanged.
- **Why:** in a real Si(100) inversion layer the electrons move into the heavy-mass valleys; one isotropic
  quantum potential cannot represent that. Measured against the anisotropic Schrodinger-Poisson oracle (made
  DOS-consistent), 1e23-1e24 m^-3, 1.0-1.3 V, Fermi-Dirac, GAMMA_WALL: N_inv **+2.9 .. +7.9 %** (N_inv >= 1e16
  m^-2; up to +14.5 % near threshold), centroid **-1.2 .. -5.2 %**. The isotropic model misses the same oracle
  by -8.7 .. -39 % / +8.8 .. +24 %.
- **`GAMMA_SI100_SP_CALIBRATED = 1.88`** (opt-in, `gamma_n=`): a labelled FIT to that oracle for the
  valley-resolved model (fit range, method and literature basis in its comment). Residuals over the 21-point
  matrix: N_inv -1.33 .. +0.93 %, centroid -2.75 .. +1.51 %. GAMMA_WALL stays the a-priori wall value.
- **Transport:** at equilibrium the full model equals the closure to 1.1e-15 with a flat quasi-Fermi level; in a
  p-n diode the currents conserve to 2e-16 and the DG change is linear in gamma; two identical sets reproduce the
  isotropic DG bit for bit. The Jacobian is exact (checked against finite differences).
- **Cost:** +2 unknowns per node per extra set (+40 % with electron DG only); see the audit note for times.
- **Defaults unchanged, byte-identical:** a new pinned call log of the bipolar DG + wall + closure path equals
  aed0aa9's; the existing digests and suites pass unchanged. A one-set table is the isotropic model bit for bit.
- **Not a default (yet):** recommended for Si(100) electron inversion, but `setup_bipolar_dg` does not know a
  region's material or orientation; the audit note measures what a default would need and move.
- **Tests / validation.** New `tests/test_valley_density_gradient.py` (on `solvers-devsim`; 16 tests, 4 @slow),
  new `validation/valley_density_gradient.py` (GATES VA / VC / VG, PASS in 444 s).

## carriers: density-gradient + closure solves 6.6-38x faster (`wip/v0151_dgperf`)

(`2026-11-10-v0.15.1-dg-iteration-cost.md`; asked for by the user after the valley-resolved-DG note found the
isotropic DG 1.2-10x slower per iteration than the larger valley-resolved model.)

- **Cause (measured): DEVSIM's bundled UMFPACK 5.1.0 (2007), not the model.** Its default strategy AUTO picks the
  "2-by-2" strategy for every density-gradient (DG) + quasi-equilibrium-closure Jacobian measured, and the numeric
  factorization then fills in: 17-43x the nonzeros of the symmetric strategy on the same ordering (2-D, electron +
  hole DG: 5.0e6 against 1.2e5; 902 ms against 6 ms), 89-97 % of a slow iteration. UMFPACK disabled that strategy in
  5.4.0 (2009). Isotropic and valley-resolved DG both get it; the valley model happened to fill in less (the luck of
  the pivot sequence), which was the whole "valley model is faster" result. Trigger: DG + closure (bisected: FD,
  quantum SRH, gamma and bias do not matter; no DG or no closure -> AUTO picks another strategy).
- **Fix: `devsim_env.umfpack_2by2_guard()`, installed by `import_devsim()`.** When AUTO's symbolic step picks
  2-by-2, it is redone with UMFPACK's symmetric strategy; every other matrix is factorized exactly as before.
  `DYNAMETA_UMFPACK_2BY2=keep` restores the old factorization. Not installed when DEVSIM uses MKL-PARDISO.
- **Speed (0 -> 0.6 -> 1.2 V MOS ramps):** isotropic DG, Windows 26.8 -> 2.8 s (1-D, hole DG), 19.8 -> 3.0 s (2-D),
  102.9 -> 3.9 s (2-D, hole DG); WSL 38.2 -> 1.9, 30.9 -> 2.2, 106.1 -> 2.8 s. Valley-resolved with the hole DG
  1.9-7.4x faster. Fixtures that did not fill in: unchanged per ramp within the run-to-run spread (the one-iteration
  probe pays one extra symbolic step per iteration, up to +6 ms). Same Newton iteration counts everywhere.
- **Results change at round-off, not bit for bit:** converged densities <= 6.5e-15 relative, potentials <= 1.1e-16 V,
  quantum potentials <= 2.1e-16 V (smaller than the old code's Windows-vs-WSL difference); the new solves are at
  least as accurate (componentwise backward error <= 1.6e-14 against up to 1.3e-12). The same applies wherever else
  AUTO chose 2-by-2 (heterojunctions, the 2-D bipolar diode: there 2-by-2 was harmless, fill within 5 %, no
  measurable time change). No call-log digest changed (they record definitions, not solves).
- **Gates:** `test_dg_closure_jacobian_factorizes_without_fill_in` (max LU fill ratio <= 4 and no 2-by-2: 2.0 new,
  80.5 old) and three guard tests in `tests/test_devsim_env.py`; all four FAIL on `30ffcd5`. No timing asserts.
- **DEVSIM side** (minimal reproduction in the audit note): not reported upstream; the user holds any DEVSIM report.
- **Tests / validation.** The DG / closure / custom-terms / API suites 125 passed on Windows and in WSL;
  the other DEVSIM suites (49 files, not slow) 712 passed / 1 xfailed (Windows), 711 passed / 1 skipped / 1 xfailed
  (WSL); validations bipolar_density_gradient, bipolar_dg_strong_inversion, valley_density_gradient PASS on both.
